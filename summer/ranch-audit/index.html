The Butterfly Ranch audit

A working example of what we do before we build anything: forty minutes walking the customer's path, then an hour in the registries. Every claim below is checked against a primary source — and where something couldn't be verified, it says so.

Verified August 26, 2026 Butterfly Rescue Ranch · Norman, OK Unsolicited — no engagement
Part one · What a stranger sees in forty minutes

The surface audit

Phone in hand, incognito, no special tools — the same walk any customer takes. The rule is evidence only: a claim needs a URL, a screenshot or a timestamp, or it doesn't get written down.

F-01

Google tells everyone they're permanently closed

The Maps listing reads "Permanently closed" — and there are two duplicate listings, both closed, at neighbouring addresses on the same road. Meanwhile the ranch is actively running summer camps and posting them on Instagram. Every stranger who searches for a horse rescue in Norman is being told this organization no longer exists.

Evidence · Google Maps search "Butterfly Ranch Norman", 12:20p Aug 26 — cross-checked against current-season Instagram activity.
F-02

You cannot give them money

The Donate button leads to a page that asks you to phone the founder. No form, no payment link, no giving levels — and across the entire site there are zero input fields and zero tappable phone numbers. For a rescue that runs on donations, the donation path doesn't exist.

Evidence · butterflyrescueranch.org/donate, 12:17p Aug 26 — page source contains no form, no payment link, no mailto. Correction: an earlier draft of this audit said donations "ride on a PayPal path" — that was wrong. The PayPal strings in the page are the site-builder's own feature flags, not a payment integration. Re-checked and corrected here.
F-03

The channels are islands

Instagram (1,072 followers) and Facebook (284) both carry the website link. The website links back to neither, and captures no email anywhere. Traffic flows one way into a page that can't do anything with it — the textbook definition of a dis-integrated system.

Evidence · Both profiles read logged-out, Aug 26 — follower counts taken from the profiles themselves, not a search result.
F-04

Search can't read the page

The browser-tab title is "Home | Butterfly Ranch" and there's no meta description on any page — so the words "Norman," "horse rescue" and "donate" never reach a search engine, and pasting the link into a text message produces a naked URL instead of a preview. The tab icon is still the site-builder's default.

Evidence · Page source of /, /about and /donate, Aug 26 — no description tag on any of the three.
F-05

The mission is already the asset

Not everything is broken. Rescued horses, a real kids' program, genuine camp photography, a 5.0-star rating, and a story no competitor in this market can copy. The site itself loads in about a second and reads fine on a phone. The problem isn't the product — it's the plumbing.

Evidence · Site loaded at 390px, ~1s; Google rating 5.0 from 6 reviews; program described on their own About page.

D-01

The buried asset: a real charity that never says so

We verified the tax exemption directly against the IRS Business Master File. Every gift to this organization is tax-deductible, and corporate matching programs would double some of them. Then we searched their entire public presence for that fact and found it nowhere — not the website, not the Donate page, not either social bio. A supporter literally cannot find the number their employer's matching-gift form asks for. They paid the full cost of becoming a charity and collect none of the return.

Evidence · IRS Business Master File (via ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer API), exemption ruled Jan 2024 · full-text search of site + both social bios, Aug 26.

D-03

Money she's eligible for and never asked for

Two funders whose published criteria she matches. One is local, funds animal wellbeing in Central Oklahoma, reviews small grants monthly, and states a vision of making Oklahoma the most humane place for animals — a kill-pen rescue serving underprivileged kids is that brief, verbatim. The other is national, gives $500–$5,000 for exactly her cost centers (vet, farrier, feed), and uses a free transparency profile as the application itself. Current grant revenue is zero — not because she was turned down, but because the prerequisite paperwork was never filed.

Evidence · Both funders' own grant pages, read Aug 26 — amounts, cycles and eligibility as published.
D-04

An open lane, and a credential to defend it

A for-profit neighbour already markets equine-assisted learning and therapy partnerships. The ranch has the charity status that schools and referral partners prefer and an instructor pursuing graduate study in equine-assisted counseling — the defensible version of that same offer, sitting completely unmarketed.

Evidence · Competitor site positioning + the ranch's own staff bios, Aug 26.
D-05

A local story with zero coverage

We searched for press and found none — not one article, ever. Consider what an assignment editor is being offered: kill-pen horses rehabilitated by a young founder who still competes in rodeo, paired with kids who may end up owning the horse they healed, with a summer of photography already shot. One pitch email is plausibly worth more reach than the ranch's entire social history.

Evidence · Press search across news and local outlets, Aug 26 — no results.
What we'd do about it

Ninety days, in order

Sequenced deliberately — each step is a prerequisite for the next, and nearly all of it is free. The expensive-looking problems turn out to be switch-flipping.

  1. Week one — stop the bleeding. Claim the Google listing, mark it open with real hours, merge the duplicate. Put the charity status and tax ID in the site footer and on the Donate page. Make the phone number tappable. Confirm the annual IRS filing is current.
  2. Weeks two to four — open the giving lane. Point the existing Donate button at one hosted giving page that issues receipts automatically. Switch the Facebook page to its nonprofit category to unlock a native donate button. Add one email field. Claim the transparency profile that grant portals check.
  3. Month two — go to market. Publish the camp page at market-anchored tiers with sponsored seats funded by donors. Launch "sponsor a horse" monthly giving at feed-bill price points. Put a QR review card in every parent's hand at camp pickup. Send the press pitch.
  4. Month three — compound. Submit the local grant letter of inquiry. Partner with a university service organization for volunteer days. Give the equine-assisted lane its own page, aimed at schools and counselors.

The one-paragraph version. "You already did the hard part — a real charity, a real program, and a story nobody in this market can copy. What's missing is plumbing: Google thinks you're closed, donors can't find your tax ID, and parents can't book the camp they'd happily pay for. Ninety days of switch-flipping, almost all of it free, connects what you built to the people trying to fund it."

How this was made

Method & honest limits

The surface audit took about forty minutes; the deep dive about an hour on top of it. No paid tools were used — the whole thing is knowing which primary source answers which question.

  • Sources · IRS Business Master File · the organization's own website, read page by page · Google Maps listings · both social profiles read directly, logged out · competitors' published pricing pages · the funders' own grant criteria pages.
  • Not claimed · Search-ranking position — an automated check was blocked partway, so no ranking claim appears anywhere in this audit. Whether the annual IRS filing is current is a question for the founder, not an assertion. The ranch's own camp pricing is unpublished, so it isn't stated.
  • Corrected in public · An earlier draft claimed donations ran through PayPal. Re-checking showed those strings were the site-builder's own feature flags. The corrected finding — that no payment path exists at all — is worse for the organization and better as an audit.
  • Standing · This is unsolicited analysis of a public-facing organization, produced as a demonstration. Nothing here is a client engagement, and no non-public information was used.
