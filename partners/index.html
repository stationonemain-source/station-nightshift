2. Sharing your link1.5 min

You don't need to cold-call anyone. Most Scouts share their link through content — a video, a post, a bio link, a DM. The link does the tracking; you just need eyes on it. You're not expected to explain all 14 products or answer technical questions.

3. What happens after you refer someone1 min

Once someone you referred is genuinely interested, they get handed off to a Partner who closes them, onboards them, and manages the account going forward. That's not a demotion of your referral — it's the model. You still get your full bounty the moment they go live and paying; you just won't be the one managing them afterward.

4. Getting your bounty1.5 min

Once someone you referred is a live, paying client — trial over if they had one — your bounty is queued for the next payout run, monthly. You'll need a tax form on file (W-9 US / W-8BEN outside the US) before your first payout clears. No cap on how many bounties you can stack.

Partner · invite-only

Partner reference material

Shown here for reference. You'll get full access once we've talked and you're approved as a Partner.

1. How the money works1.5 min

One number: 40%, recurring, on every client in your book, for as long as they stay. Calculated on cleared invoices, never list price. Annual prepays spread across 12 months. If a client starts on a trial, your commission starts once it ends and their first invoice clears. No bounty on top, no tier to climb toward.

2. Your book of business2 min

When a client becomes yours — whether you closed them yourself or a Scout's warm lead was distributed to you — they're yours going forward. You own the full relationship: every upsell conversation, every general question, every support request that isn't a technical break/fix. Think of it like an insurance agent's book of business — tracked as permanently assigned to you. Leaving the program requires written notice; your book is then redistributed.

3. What to lead with, by business type3 min

  • Answers their own phone → Frontdesk ($397–$897)
  • No real website / ugly one → Storefront ($500+$49/mo)
  • Unanswered 1-2 star reviews → Repute ($197)
  • Already asking about "the AI phone thing" → Lineback ($197–$297)

If you're not sure, ask what's costing them the most right now.

4. Use the demo tool, don't pitch blind2.5 min

Before you call a business, go to Demo sites, enter their name and city, and Station builds a real working example for THAT business plus a specific dollar figure for what their gap is costing them. Then you're showing something real, not pitching an idea.

5. The call script pattern3 min

Confirm who you're talking to, fast. State the specific gap you found, with a number if you have one. Offer to text the demo link live, on the call. You're not closing the sale — Station's free audit does that. Your only job is to get them to look at the demo.

6. Handling objections2.5 min

"We already have a receptionist" → catches what they can't, nights and weekends. "Sounds expensive" → the audit's free and shows the real number first. "Sounds too good to be true" → that's why there's a real demo instead of just being told. Redirect to something they can verify, never argue.

7. Compliance, taxes, and what never to say3.5 min

No income claims, ever. No unauthorized branding or landing pages. You represent what Station does, you don't promise a result. W-9/W-8BEN on file before your first payout. Never name the platform Station is built on, and never state a stat you haven't personally verified.
