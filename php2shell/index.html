Security notice · PHP2Shell

A big PHP flaw dropped this week. Most small-business sites run on PHP.

On 21 October 2026, the people who maintain PHP shipped an emergency fix for two serious bugs. If your website runs on PHP — and most do, including nearly every WordPress site — here is what it means in plain words, and how to find out in two minutes whether you are exposed.

Last updated 21 October 2026 · written for business owners, not engineers

What actually happened

Two flaws were found in PHP itself — the software that runs the back end of a huge share of the web. Nicknamed PHP2Shell, they are tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, and both are rated 9.2 out of 10 for severity. That is about as serious as these ratings go.

In plain terms: under the right conditions, an attacker on the internet could make a PHP website run commands it was never meant to run — without any password. That is the kind of hole used to deface a site, steal what is in its database, or quietly plant malware that infects your visitors.

The details leaked a day early, on 20 October, so the PHP team released the fix ahead of schedule on the 21st. That is good news: the patch exists. The risk now is every site that has not applied it yet.

Are you exposed?

You almost certainly run PHP if any of these is true. You do not need to know the technical details — the point is simply that PHP is probably involved.

  • Your site is built on WordPress. WordPress is PHP. This is the single most common case for small businesses.
  • You are on shared or cPanel hosting — GoDaddy, Bluehost, HostGator, Hostinger, Namecheap and the like. These almost all serve PHP.
  • Your site uses Joomla, Drupal, Magento or an older custom build. All PHP.
  • You are not sure what your site is built on. That by itself is worth a two-minute check.

Two reassuring exceptions: sites built on Squarespace, Wix, Shopify or Webflow are run by those companies, who patch their own servers — you do not have to do anything. And a site that is plain static pages, with no PHP at all, is not affected by this.

What to do

  1. Find out if your site runs PHP, and which version. If you are on a managed builder (Squarespace, Wix, Shopify), you are covered — stop here. Otherwise, keep going.
  2. Ask your host or web person to confirm the patch is applied. The fixed versions are 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35. A good host will have done this automatically; many have not. It is a fair question to ask them today.
  3. If your PHP version is older than 8.2, you have a bigger problem. Those versions are end-of-life and will never be patched. The real fix is to move off them — which, for most small sites, is a good idea anyway.
  4. Keep your plugins and themes updated. On WordPress, outdated add-ons are the usual way these holes actually get exploited. Update them, and remove any you do not use.

Common questions

Is my website affected?
If it runs on WordPress or sits on ordinary shared hosting, PHP is involved, so the flaw applies until your host confirms the patch. If you are on Squarespace, Wix, Shopify or Webflow, those companies run the servers and handle it for you. Run our free check if you are not sure which you have.
Does this mean I have already been hacked?
No. A flaw being published is not the same as your site being broken into. It means the door could be opened if it is left unpatched. The sensible move is to confirm the patch and tighten the obvious things, not to panic.
